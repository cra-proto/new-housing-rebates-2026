The GST/HST new housing rebate allows an individual to recover some of the GST or the federal part of the HST paid for a new or substantially renovated house that is for use as the individual's, or their relation's, primary place of residence, when all of the other conditions are met.

In addition, other provincial new housing rebates may be available for the provincial part of the HST whether or not the rebate for the federal part of the HST is available.

Who can apply

You may be eligible for a new housing rebate for some of the GST/HST paid if:

  • You are an individual
    If there are multiple owners

    If there is more than one owner of the house, all of the owners must be individuals.

    Partnerships (even if all the partners are individuals) and corporations are not eligible to claim this rebate. An individual cannot claim this rebate if a partnership or a corporation is also an owner of the housing.

    If more than one individual owns the housing or share, only one individual can claim the GST/HST new housing rebate.

  • You did one of the following:

    • You purchased a home from a builder and bought or leased the land under the same written agreement

      Lease details

      The lease for the land with the builder must give you the option to purchase the land or be at least 20 years long.

    • You, or someone you hired, built a home on land you already owned or leased

    • You purchased a share in a cooperative housing corporation (co-op)

    If it's a mobile home

  • The home is new or substantially renovated
    What is substantial renovation

    In a substantial renovation, the interior of a house is essentially gutted.

    A renovation of your basement, or adding a garage or a deck to your existing house, by itself, would not meet the definition of a substantial renovation and you would not qualify for the new housing rebate.

    The 90% test

    Generally, 90% or more of the interior of an existing house is the minimum that has to be removed or replaced to qualify as a substantial renovation (the 90% test).

    Livable areas

    Only livable areas count towards a substantial renovation, including finished basements and finished attics.

    Livable areas do not include garages or crawl spaces. Work done to partially complete a basement but not make it a livable basement does not count toward the 90% test.

    However, if all or part of an unfinished basement was renovated into a livable area, this area would be taken into account in applying the 90% test.

    Determine if you meet the 90% test

    You can use any fair and reasonable method to determine if you meet the 90% test. For example, you can:

    • Compare the square metres of the renovated livable floor space to the total square metres of livable floor space of the house
    • Compare the square metres of the floor and wall space of the areas renovated to the total floor and wall space of the house

    Major addition

    A major addition to a house may qualify when you are also substantially renovating the existing house. The addition should at least double the size of the livable areas of your existing house, which is absorbed into the new one.

    The work must be so extensive that the result is viewed as a newly built house. An example would be making significant structural changes to the appearance and layout of an existing bungalow and adding a full second story.

    An addition, by itself, is not considered a substantial renovation. Significant physical and functional changes also have to be made to the existing house so that the resulting structure would be seen as a newly constructed house.

    For example, if a 700 square metres bungalow is being renovated and a 100 square metres addition is added, the 90% test does not consider the addition. However, if the renovation of the 700 square metres bungalow is found to be a substantial renovation, the GST/HST paid on the addition is also eligible for the GST/HST new housing rebate.

    Conversion

    If you convert a property from non-residential use into your house, it is considered a substantial renovation even if the extent of the work does not pass the 90% test.

    As long as you meet all of the other eligibility conditions, you may qualify for the rebate.

    If you purchase the building, the rebate is available for a part of any GST/HST paid on the purchase and other improvements made to convert the building.

    If you held or used the building as capital property in your business, and you begin to use the building as your residence, you are considered to have sold and repurchased the property. You must pay the GST/HST to the CRA, calculated on the fair market value of the property. You can include the GST/HST that you paid to the CRA, plus the GST/HST you paid in improvements, in your rebate calculation.

    If you paid the GST/HST when you first purchased the building to hold or use as capital property in your business and you were not entitled to claim an input tax credit (ITC) for all or part of the GST/HST you paid on the purchase or on any improvements you made to the building before you began to use it as your residence, you may be entitled to claim an ITC or a rebate.

  • The home is intended to be your, or your relation's, primary place of residence
    What primary place of residence means

    Your primary place of residence is generally a house that you own, jointly or otherwise, and that you intend to live in on a permanent basis. You may have more than one place of residence, but you are considered to have only one primary place of residence.

    CRA may consider the following to determine whether a house is your or your relation's primary place of residence for purposes of the new housing rebate:

    • Whether you consider the house as your main residence
    • The length of time you inhabit the premises
    • The designation of that address on personal and public records such as:
      • Mailing address
      • Income tax (for example, forms or returns)
      • Voting
      • Municipal or school taxes

    Your intent to use the house as your primary place of residence must be evident at the outset of purchasing, constructing, or substantially renovating the house.

    A house is not considered your primary place of residence if:

    • You intend to use the house as your primary place of residence some time in the future, such as retirement
    • The house is a recreational cottage or an investment property
    Primary residence if there are multiple owners

    If two or more individuals are sharing ownership of the house, the rebate may be available if the house will be the primary place of residence of:

    • Any of the individuals
    • A relation of any of the individuals

    All the other conditions for the rebate must be met by at least one of the individuals.

    Only one of the individuals may apply for the GST/HST new housing rebate (that is, only one can be the claimant).

  • You are the first resident of the home
    Exception: Selling the house before anyone occupied it

    If you had originally intended that the house would be your primary place of residence, but you sold the house and transferred the ownership to the purchaser before you, or anyone else, occupied it, you may still be eligible to claim a new housing rebate if the sale of the house is considered an exempt sale.

    Exempt sale

    Generally, your sale of a house will be exempt if:

    • You are not considered to be a builder of the house for GST/HST purposes
      Determining if you are a builder for GST/HST purposes
    • You did not claim any input tax credits for the GST/HST payable on costs incurred to build or substantially renovate the house
  • You meet the applicable maximums for purchase price or fair market value (FMV)
    Purchase price or FMV maximums for most provinces and territories

    The purchase price or FMV is less than:

    You bought a home from a builder or a share in a co-op

    • If you bought the land: Option 1

      $450,000 before tax, including the purchase price for a taxable assignment to you of the original purchase and sale agreement (if applicable)

    • If you lease the land: Option 2

      $472,500 when possession was transferred to you, if the builder had to account for the GST at 5% on the self supply of the house

    If you built your own home

    $450,000 at the time that the work is substantially completed, including the land, building and any other structures

    If you live in certain other provinces

    You may be looking for:

    • Ontario

    The FMV or purchase price of the home must be less than:

    You bought a home from a builder or a share in a co-op

    • If you bought the land: Option 1

      $450,000 before tax, including the purchase price for a taxable assignment to you of the original purchase and sale agreement (if applicable)

    • If you lease the land: Option 2

      $513,000
      If the house is located in Nova Scotia (and both possession and ownership of the house were transferred on or after April 1, 2025), and the builder or co-op had to account for the HST at 14%
      $517,500
      If the house is located in Nova Scotia (and either possession or ownership was transferred to you before April 1, 2025), New Brunswick, Newfoundland and Labrador, or Prince Edward Island, and the builder or co-op had to account for the HST at 15% on the self-supply of the house

    You built your own home

    $450,000 at the time that the work is substantially completed, including the land, building and any other structures

Ontario GST/HST new housing rebate

For houses located in Ontario, an Ontario new housing rebate may be available even if the total purchase price or FMV of the house is above the applicable maximum.

To claim the Ontario new housing rebate, you must meet all of the other eligibility criteria for the GST/HST new housing rebate.
